<what_to_include>
- Show only the listed ingredients, in their finished, cooked state. Add no unlisted garnish, herbs, seasoning, oil or sauce. - For each visible component give its form (whole, sliced, cubed, strands, mound, layer, pool), a specific colour, its cooked surface or texture, and its position relative to the other components.
- Use precise colour words that match a well-made version of the dish, for example golden-amber, brick-orange, pale cream, deep green, chestnut. Do not write plain "brown" for a sauce or crust, and do not make sauces muddy or dark.
- Keep proteins whole if the dish is served whole. Show cut pieces if the dish calls for them: slices where the dish is customarily sliced, chunks coated in sauce in a curry or stew.
- Sauce placement: put sauce beside or under the main item unless the customary dish is served with the sauce poured over it. If a sauce partly covers an item, say what remains exposed. State sauce thickness.
- Listed toppings such as grated cheese, spring onion or sesame stay visible on top of the dish.
- Use quantities only to set relative portion sizes. Do not state weights or volumes. State an exact piece count only when the recipe establishes it for one serving; otherwise describe the portion by shape or arrangement.
- Include natural home-cooked variation (slight unevenness, irregular edges), not factory uniformity.
- Mention the serving vessel only if the recipe specifies it or it is customary for the dish, in one plain phrase.
</what_to_include>
